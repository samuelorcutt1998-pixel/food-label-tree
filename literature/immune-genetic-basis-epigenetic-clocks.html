Decoding the immune-genetic basis of epigenetic clocks: evidence from single-cell transcriptome-wide Mendelian randomization and colocalization

Dongren Sun, Xin Mo, Shuguang Zou, Jianjun Zou, Zhihong Zhao

Epigenetics & Chromatin · 6 Nov 2026 · Research

← Back to the Longevity course

Read the original paper →doi:10.1186/s13072-026-00711-z

Open access. The full paper is free to read.

Genetic analyses tied immune-cell gene activity, including DNA-damage-response genes such as ATM, to widely used DNA-methylation aging clocks in a cell-type-specific way.

Why it matters

Epigenetic clocks are widely used in aging research because their DNA-methylation scores track disease risk and mortality. What they do not reveal on their own is which genes, in which cells, help set those scores. Immune cells are a natural place to look: blood is the tissue most clocks are built from, immune composition changes with age, and some clock signals overlap with inflammation and white-cell mix. If gene activity inside particular immune cell types can be linked to clock values through inherited genetic variation, that would point toward mechanisms rather than mere correlation. This paper asks whether the expression of specific genes in defined immune cells is associated with four DNA-methylation clocks.

What they did

The authors combined single-cell cis-expression quantitative trait loci from the OneK1K cohort, which link inherited DNA variants to gene activity inside particular immune cell types, with two-sample Mendelian randomization and Bayesian colocalization. They tested genetic associations between immune-cell-specific gene expression and four DNA-methylation clocks, then tried to replicate the lead associations in GTEx V11 whole-blood expression data and the TenK10K immune-cell eQTL dataset. Follow-up analyses included functional enrichment, protein-protein interaction networks, a hierarchical target-assessment framework, and phenome-wide association and druggability checks meant to highlight genes of possible therapeutic interest.

What they found

According to the abstract, they identified 22 significant associations between an expressed gene and an immune cell type. Of these, 14 showed strong colocalization evidence, with a posterior probability for a shared causal variant (PPH4) of 0.8 or higher, and 8 showed suggestive colocalization (PPH4 between 0.5 and 0.8). All 22 associations passed Steiger filtering, a check that the genetic effect is more consistent with gene expression influencing the clock than the reverse. Replication in GTEx V11 and TenK10K confirmed the direction of effect for most of the lead associations, especially for ENO1, NDUFS5, ATM, and PPP1R17. Functional enrichment pointed to DNA damage response, immune regulation, and metabolic pathways. The authors say phenome-wide and druggability assessments prioritized genes with therapeutic potential, and they conclude that immune-cell gene expression influences epigenetic aging in a cell-type-specific manner.

Limits

This synopsis was written from the abstract only. The article is open access under a CC BY-NC-ND license, but the full text could not be retrieved during this sweep, so details beyond the abstract are not included. Mendelian randomization depends on assumptions about genetic instruments; leftover pleiotropy can still bias results. The abstract does not name the four clocks, report effect sizes, or describe the ancestry mix of the cohorts, so the strength and generalizability of each association cannot be judged here. A genetic association is not the same as an experiment that changes a gene’s activity and watches the clock move.

Key terms

Epigenetic clock
A statistical model that estimates biological age from DNA methylation levels at selected CpG sites.
Mendelian randomization
A method that uses inherited DNA variants as natural experiments to test whether an exposure, such as a gene’s activity, is likely to influence an outcome.
cis-eQTL
A DNA variant near a gene that is associated with how much of that gene is expressed, here measured inside a specific immune cell type.
Colocalization
A statistical test of whether the same genetic variant likely drives two traits, such as gene expression and a clock score, rather than two nearby but distinct variants.
ATM
A gene that helps cells sense DNA breaks and coordinate repair; inherited variation in ATM is a classic link between genome maintenance and disease.
Steiger filter
A check used in Mendelian randomization to test whether the data are more consistent with the proposed direction of effect than with the reverse.
