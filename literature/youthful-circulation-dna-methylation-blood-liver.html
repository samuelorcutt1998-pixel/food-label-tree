Youthful circulation reshapes age-associated DNA methylation across blood and liver

Igor Kovalchuk, Olga Kovalchuk

The Journals of Gerontology, Series A · 12 Nov 2026 · Research

← Back to the Longevity course

Read the original paper →doi:10.1093/gerona/glag258

Open access. The full paper is free to read.

Re-analysing a mouse parabiosis study, the authors find that sharing a young blood supply pushes tens of thousands of age-linked DNA methylation sites back toward a younger state in both blood and liver, and many stay reversed two months later.

Why it matters

Epigenetic clocks read age from methyl tags on DNA, and earlier work showed that joining an old mouse to a young one for a long time (heterochronic parabiosis) lowers the old animal's DNA methylation age. That is one of the clearest signs that epigenetic aging can be pushed backward. What was not clear is how this reversal is laid out across the genome, whether different tissues respond at the same spots, and whether the effect lasts once the young blood supply is removed. Those details matter for judging whether blood-borne factors could ever be a real rejuvenation strategy, and for understanding what an epigenetic clock is actually measuring when it 'goes backward'.

What they did

The authors did not run a new animal experiment. They re-analysed processed methylation data (reduced-representation bisulfite sequencing, a method that reads methylation at many CpG sites) from 78 blood and liver samples in a previously published prolonged-parabiosis study. They kept just over one million CpG sites that were well covered in every sample. For each site they measured how methylation changes with normal aging and how it changes after parabiosis, and called a site 'reversed' when parabiosis moved it in the opposite direction to aging by a set amount (a 10-percentage-point threshold as the main cut-off). They checked whether the same exact sites were reversed in both tissues and whether reversal persisted after the mice were separated. They used statistical null models that respect chromosome structure to test whether overlaps and gene-level patterns were more than chance.

What they found

Parabiosis-linked methylation changes ran against normal aging in both blood and liver, including in comparisons designed to avoid relying on one shared reference group. Right after parabiosis, about 22,400 blood sites and 22,300 liver sites were reversed, with 1,792 shared at identical positions. Two months after separation, about 25,900 blood and 15,400 liver sites were reversed, with 1,387 shared. A core of 64 sites was reversed in both tissues at both time points. Reversed sites were less common in gene promoters and CpG islands and more common inside gene bodies and in 'open-sea' regions away from islands. Tissue-specific reversed sites showed a modest tendency to fall near the same genes, but tests for convergence on promoters, gene bodies or biological pathways did not hold up under stricter null models.

Limits

This synopsis is based on the abstract only, although the article is open access. The work is a secondary analysis of one earlier mouse experiment, so it inherits that study's design and sample size and cannot test new conditions. Parabiosis is a surgical model that shares far more than blood factors, so it does not identify which components drive the effect. The method samples only part of the genome. The 'reversed' counts depend on the chosen threshold. The abstract does not show whether these methylation changes alter gene activity or health, and mouse results may not apply to people.

Key terms

Heterochronic parabiosis
Surgically joining an old and a young animal so they share a circulation.
DNA methylation age
An age estimate calculated from methyl tags at selected DNA sites.
CpG site
A spot in DNA where C is followed by G; the usual place methyl tags sit.
CpG island
A stretch of DNA dense in CpG sites, often near gene starts (promoters).
Open sea
Parts of the genome far from CpG islands.
Null model
A statistical baseline describing what overlap would look like by chance.
