Aging out of the blue: region-specific epigenetic clock calibration for a blue zone with the DNAm SuperLearner

Nolan Gunter et al.

PLOS Computational Biology · 18 Nov 2026 · Research

← Back to the Longevity course

Read the original paper →doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014949

Open access. The full paper is free to read.

Off-the-shelf epigenetic clocks disagreed by about seven years on Costa Rica's Nicoya blue zone; after careful local calibration only a small biological-age advantage remained.

Why it matters

Epigenetic clocks estimate biological age from chemical methyl marks on DNA at specific CpG sites, and the gap between that estimate and calendar age, called epigenetic age acceleration (EAA), predicts disease and death. 'Blue zones' are regions where unusually many people live long lives, so they are natural places to ask whether residents age more slowly at the molecular level. But most clocks are trained on large mixed populations, so they reflect average trends and may be biased in a particular region. If different clocks give different answers, conclusions about a blue zone could depend more on the tool than on the people.

What they did

Using data from the Costa Rican Longevity and Healthy Aging Study (CRELES), the researchers analysed DNA methylation in 206 residents of the Nicoya blue zone and 875 people from elsewhere in Costa Rica. They trained a SuperLearner, a machine-learning method that finds the best weighted combination of many existing clocks and flexible algorithms, on the non-Nicoyan group only. They then used it to build a calibrated statistical test comparing EAA between Nicoya and the reference population, and compared results before and after calibration, including calibration within 10-year age groups.

What they found

Before calibration or adjustment, the answer depended heavily on the clock. The SuperLearner placed Nicoyans about three years younger on average (−3.05 years, 95% CI −3.64 to −2.46), while the DNAmFitAge clock placed them almost four years older (+3.59 years, 95% CI 3.02 to 4.17). Calibrating each clock's average to the non-Nicoyan Costa Ricans made the clocks agree, with an estimated EAA advantage of about two years for Nicoya (−1.96 years, 95% CI −2.56 to −1.37). After calibrating within 10-year age groups, only a small advantage remained (−0.35 years, 95% CI −0.64 to −0.05), concentrated among the oldest participants. The authors offer the approach as a reproducible framework for comparing regions when a suitable reference population exists.

Limits

This synopsis is based on the abstract only. The article is listed as open access, but its full text was not reviewed for this page, so sample sizes, effect sizes and methods details are not given here. The study is cross-sectional and measures methylation once, so it cannot show that Nicoyans age more slowly over time or explain why. Results depend on having a well-matched reference population, and the Nicoya sample is modest in size. The paper mainly addresses how to measure fairly; the small remaining difference shows that headline claims of much younger biological age in blue zones can shrink with careful calibration.

Key terms

Epigenetic clock
A model that estimates age from DNA methylation at selected CpG sites.
Epigenetic age acceleration (EAA)
How much older or younger a person's epigenetic age is than expected for their calendar age.
Blue zone
A region with unusually high rates of long life, such as Nicoya in Costa Rica.
SuperLearner
An ensemble machine-learning method that combines many predictors into the best weighted mix.
Calibration
Adjusting a model's outputs so they match a reference population, removing systematic bias.
95% confidence interval (CI)
The range within which the true value is likely to lie, given the data.
