TRAF1 promotes age-related cardiac dysfunction through a three-step mechanism: cardiomyocyte senescence, mitochondrial DNA release, and subsequent macrophage-driven inflammation

Wei Yang et al.

Cell Biology and Toxicology · 13 Nov 2026 · Research

← Back to the Longevity course

Read the original paper →doi:10.1007/s10565-026-10308-7

Open access. The full paper is free to read.

In aged mice, a protein called TRAF1 pushed heart muscle cells to leak mitochondrial DNA that set off inflammation, and lowering it improved heart relaxation.

Why it matters

Heart failure becomes far more common with age, and part of the reason is thought to be chronic, low-grade inflammation. One proposed trigger is DNA from mitochondria, the cell's power units, escaping into places it should not be. Immune sensors such as cGAS-STING treat stray DNA as a danger signal and switch on inflammation. Finding which molecules cause aging heart cells to release mitochondrial DNA could point to new treatment targets for age-related heart decline.

What they did

The researchers first searched public gene-expression datasets (GEO) for genes that change with cardiac aging, which pointed to TRAF1 (TNF receptor-associated factor 1). In naturally aged mice of both sexes (90 weeks old), they reduced TRAF1 only in heart muscle cells using a virus (AAV9) carrying a heart-specific silencing construct, then examined the hearts at 110 weeks. In the lab, they treated heart muscle cells with D-galactose, a chemical used to mimic aging, and either reduced or boosted TRAF1. They measured reactive oxygen species (ROS), leakage of mitochondrial DNA, macrophage activation and inflammation.

What they found

TRAF1 was strongly increased in aged and failing hearts. In cells, lowering TRAF1 reduced D-galactose-induced senescence and ROS build-up, while raising it made both worse. TRAF1 switched on NF-kB signalling and increased Nox2, an enzyme that makes ROS, which promoted release of mitochondrial DNA from senescent heart cells. That released DNA activated the cGAS-STING pathway in macrophages, amplifying inflammation in the heart. In aged mice, heart-specific TRAF1 knockdown lowered cardiac ROS, reduced inflammatory macrophages in the heart and pro-inflammatory cytokines in blood, eased heart enlargement and improved diastolic function (the heart's ability to relax and fill), without changing ejection fraction.

Limits

This synopsis is based on the abstract only; the article is listed as open access but its full text was not reviewed here. The work is in mice and cultured cells, and D-galactose is an artificial stand-in for natural aging. Knocking down a gene with a virus is a research tool, not a therapy, and no drug targeting TRAF1 was tested. Pumping function (ejection fraction) did not change, so the benefit was limited to relaxation and structure. Whether the same chain of events drives heart aging in people is not shown.

Key terms

Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)
The small separate genome inside mitochondria; when released into the cell or outside it, it can trigger immune alarms.
cGAS-STING
An immune sensing pathway that detects DNA in the wrong place and switches on inflammation.
Cellular senescence
A state in which damaged cells stop dividing but stay active and release inflammatory signals.
NF-kB
A master switch protein that turns on many inflammation-related genes.
Diastolic function
How well the heart relaxes and fills with blood between beats.
