Valproic acid, but not lithium, alters telomere homeostasis and telomerase activity in a neuronal in vitro model

Johanna Thurin, Arthna Rathes, Lou-Ann Marivin, Alicja Puszkiel, Xavier Decleves, Frank Bellivier, Bruno Etain, Cynthia Marie-Claire

Journal of Psychopharmacology · 20 Nov 2026 · Research

In lab-grown neuron-like cells, the mood stabiliser valproic acid, but not lithium, reshaped telomere maintenance genes and cut telomerase activity after three weeks, without changing telomere length yet.

Why it matters

Telomeres are protective DNA caps at the ends of chromosomes. They shorten over a lifetime and are used as a biomarker of cellular ageing. People with bipolar disorder tend to have shorter telomeres than average. Telomere length is maintained by three protein complexes, shelterin, which shields the telomere, the telomerase enzyme, which can lengthen it, and the CST complex, plus several helper proteins. Lithium and valproic acid are two of the most commonly prescribed mood stabilisers for bipolar disorder, and some earlier work suggested lithium might protect telomeres. But how either drug affects the whole telomere maintenance system, at doses similar to those used in treatment, had not been studied.

What they did

The researchers grew SH-SY5Y cells, an immortalised human cell line often used as a stand-in for neurons, with lithium (1 mM) or valproic acid (0.48 mM), concentrations chosen to match therapeutic levels, for 7, 14 and 21 days. They measured the activity of 18 genes involved in telomere upkeep, levels of the telomerase catalytic protein TERT, telomerase enzyme activity, and telomere length.

What they found

Lithium had no effect on any part of the telomere system. Valproic acid affected the whole system by day 21. It raised the activity of two of the six shelterin genes, lowered four of the seven telomerase-complex genes including TERT, pushed two CST complex genes in opposite directions, and lowered the helper genes RTEL1 and APOLLO. It also reduced TERT protein in the cell nucleus and reduced telomerase activity at day 21. Telomere length itself did not change over the three weeks. In other words, valproic acid turned down the machinery that maintains telomeres before any measurable shortening had appeared.

Limits

This synopsis is based on the abstract only, because the full text is paywalled. The cells are an immortalised cancer-derived line, which, as the authors note, limits how directly the results apply to real neurons. The experiment lasted only 21 days, which may be too short for telomere length to change. Only one dose of each drug was tested, and there were no patient data, so the study cannot say whether valproic acid affects telomeres or ageing in people taking it.

Key terms

Telomere
A repeating DNA sequence that caps and protects chromosome ends.
Telomerase
An enzyme that adds DNA repeats to telomeres; TERT is its catalytic part.
Shelterin
A six-protein complex that binds and protects telomeres.
CST complex
A protein complex that helps finish copying telomere DNA and limits telomerase.
SH-SY5Y
A human neuroblastoma cell line widely used as a model of neurons.
