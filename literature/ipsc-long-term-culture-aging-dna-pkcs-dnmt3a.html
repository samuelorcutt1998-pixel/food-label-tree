DNA-PKcs-DNMT3A axis triggers aging-like deteriorations in mouse pluripotent stem cells

Xiaoyu Xu et al.

Advanced Science · 18 Nov 2026 · Research

← Back to the Longevity course

Read the original paper →doi:10.1002/advs.78797

Open access. The full paper is free to read.

Stem cells thought to be 'ageless' lost DNA-repair capacity and identity after long culture, through a chain running from lamin A to the repair enzyme DNA-PKcs to the methylation enzyme DNMT3A.

Why it matters

Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) are adult cells reprogrammed back to an embryo-like state. They can, in principle, become any cell type, which makes them a major hope for regenerative medicine, including treatments for age-related diseases. They are often described as ageless because they can keep dividing in the lab without obvious decline. But if iPSCs quietly accumulate damage while they are grown for long periods, the cells used to make therapies could be of lower quality than assumed. The question also matters for ageing biology in general, because it touches the links between DNA repair, the epigenome and loss of cell identity, three processes that go wrong in ageing tissues.

What they did

The researchers grew mouse iPSCs for long periods with repeated passaging and compared long-term cultured cells with earlier-passage cells. They examined DNA repair through non-homologous end joining (NHEJ), the main pathway that rejoins broken DNA strands, along with markers of pluripotent identity, the ability to differentiate into other cell types and genome-wide gene expression. They then traced the molecular steps involved, focusing on the nuclear scaffold protein lamin A, the repair enzyme DNA-PKcs, the transcription factors SP1/3 and the DNA methylation enzyme DNMT3A.

What they found

Long-term cultured iPSCs developed what the authors call ageing-like decline: impaired NHEJ repair, loss of cellular identity and reduced ability to differentiate. The proposed chain of events starts with falling levels of lamin A, which leads to inactivation of DNA-PKcs and therefore poorer NHEJ repair. Inactive DNA-PKcs in turn reduced expression of DNMT3A, acting through SP1/3, and this was accompanied by gene-expression patterns typical of ageing and by weakened pluripotency. The authors describe this as crosstalk between DNA damage and the epigenome, argue that genome stability should be monitored in iPSCs destined for the clinic, and say they have identified targets that could be used to improve cell quality.

Limits

This synopsis is based on the abstract only. The article is listed as open access, but its full text was not reviewed for this page, so sample sizes, effect sizes and methods details are not given here. The work is in mouse cells grown in culture, so it is unclear how closely the changes match ageing in a living body or in human iPSCs. 'Ageing-like' is the authors' description of a set of molecular and functional changes, not proof that the cells age in the same way tissues do. The abstract does not say how many passages were needed, how large the effects were, or whether the suggested targets have been tested to restore cell quality.

Key terms

Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)
Adult cells reprogrammed into an embryo-like state that can become many cell types.
Passaging
Repeatedly splitting and re-plating cells to keep them growing in the lab.
Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)
The main pathway that directly rejoins the two ends of a broken DNA double strand.
DNA-PKcs
A key kinase enzyme that drives NHEJ repair.
Lamin A
A protein that forms part of the scaffold lining the inside of the nucleus; its loss is linked to premature ageing.
DNMT3A
An enzyme that adds new methyl marks to DNA, shaping which genes are on or off.
