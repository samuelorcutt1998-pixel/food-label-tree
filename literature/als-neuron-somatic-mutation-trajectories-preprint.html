Altered age-dependent somatic mutational trajectories in ALS motor cortex neurons

Liyueyue Liu, Charlotte van Dijk, Sam Bonsall, Rodrigo Siqueira Kazu, Sai Zhang, Johnathan Cooper-Knock, Jan Veldink, Kevin Patrick Kenna

bioRxiv (preprint) · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint

Preprint, not yet peer reviewed. These results have not been checked by independent reviewers and may change.

Read the original paper →doi:10.64898/2026.11.17.767052

Open access. The full paper is free to read.

Preprint: motor-cortex neurons from younger ALS patients carried more somatic mutations than controls of the same age, a gap that narrowed in older donors.

Why it matters

Every cell picks up new DNA changes, called somatic mutations, over a lifetime. In neurons, which rarely or never divide, these mutations build up steadily with age, and they have been proposed as one link between ageing and why some neurons become vulnerable to disease. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is a fatal disease in which motor neurons die. Its onset depends on genes, environment and age, but the age component is poorly understood. If neurons in people with ALS accumulate mutations differently from neurons in healthy people, that could point to a mechanism connecting genome damage, ageing and neurodegeneration.

What they did

Instead of generating new data, the researchers reanalysed three existing single-nucleus datasets from ALS and control motor cortex that combined RNA sequencing and ATAC sequencing (a map of open, accessible DNA) using the 10X platform. They called variants within the parts of the genome those methods cover, checked that the calls behaved like known neuronal somatic mutations, and compared mutation burden and mutational signatures across age in ALS versus controls. They then looked for the same pattern in an independent dataset of single-cell whole-genome sequencing.

What they found

The variant calls showed the expected hallmarks of real neuronal somatic mutations: they were enriched in neurons, increased with age and included SBS5, a 'clock-like' mutational signature that accumulates steadily over life. In ALS, neuronal mutation burden followed a different age trajectory from controls. Younger ALS donors had a higher burden, and the difference shrank in older donors. SBS5 was the largest fitted component and showed a matching age-dependent difference. A similar pattern appeared in the independent whole-genome dataset. The authors conclude that ALS is associated with age-dependent changes in neuronal somatic mutation patterns.

Limits

This is a preprint and has not been peer reviewed, so its findings may change. This synopsis is based on the abstract only. The analysis reuses data that were not designed for mutation calling and covers only the genomic regions captured by these methods, which may miss or misclassify some mutations. The abstract does not give donor numbers or effect sizes. The finding is an association: it does not show whether extra mutations help cause ALS or result from the disease process.

Key terms

Somatic mutation
A DNA change that arises in a body cell after conception and is not inherited.
ALS
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, a fatal disease in which motor neurons degenerate.
Single-nucleus RNA/ATAC sequencing
Methods that read gene activity and open-DNA regions in individual cell nuclei.
Mutational signature
A characteristic pattern of DNA changes left by a particular mutational process.
SBS5
A 'clock-like' single-base-substitution signature that builds up steadily with age in many tissues.
Mutation burden
The number of somatic mutations in a cell or sample.
